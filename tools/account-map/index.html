The account map

NRE, NRO, FCNR or RFC — which account for which money?

Four Indian accounts, each built for a different kind of money, each with its own tax and its own moment to convert. Put money in the wrong one and you either pay tax you needn't or trip a FEMA rule. Here's which is which — and what has to change when your status does.

What changes when your status does

Accounts don't convert themselves, and the duty is yours, not the bank's. Two moments matter.

Going to sea
Becoming an NRI
  • Your old resident savings account must be redesignated as an NRO account within a reasonable period of becoming non-resident.
  • Open an NRE account for salary you want in rupees, and an FCNR(B) deposit for earnings you'd rather keep in foreign currency.
  • Not redesignating is a FEMA breach — banks increasingly freeze such accounts on FATCA / CRS data matching.
Coming ashore
Becoming a resident
  • Redesignate NRE and NRO to resident accounts promptly on return — under FEMA you're resident from the day you land.
  • Open an RFC account so your foreign currency doesn't get force-converted to rupees; its interest is tax-free through your RNOR window.
  • Let FCNR and NRE fixed deposits run to maturity, then move them to RFC or a resident deposit — don't break them early.
  • The timing turns on your RNOR window — sequence the conversions against the date it closes.

Common questions

My salary is in USD — where should it land? +
Into an NRE account if you want it in rupees to spend or invest in India, or an FCNR(B) deposit if you'd rather hold the dollars and hedge the rupee. Both keep the interest tax-free while you're an NRI. The one place it shouldn't land is an old resident savings account or an NRO — that muddies a clean position and, for NRO, makes the interest taxable.
Can I keep my NRE account after I move back to India? +
No. Under FEMA you're resident from the day you return to settle, and the NRE account must be redesignated to a resident account or its balance moved to RFC — promptly, not "when you get around to it." The tax-free treatment of NRE interest is tied to your being a non-resident under FEMA, so it ends when your status changes, whether or not you've told the bank.
What's the difference between FCNR and RFC? +
FCNR(B) is a non-resident product — a foreign-currency term deposit you hold while you're an NRI. RFC is its resident cousin — a foreign-currency account you open after you've come home, to keep dollars or pounds without force-converting to rupees. When an FCNR deposit matures after you've returned, you can't renew it as FCNR; the proceeds go into RFC or a resident deposit. RFC interest stays tax-free through your RNOR window.
How much can I send abroad from my NRO account? +
Up to USD 1 million per financial year from your NRO balances, after the applicable Indian tax and with the paperwork — a chartered accountant's certificate in Form 15CB and Form 15CA. NRE and FCNR balances, by contrast, are fully and freely repatriable with no such cap.
Do I have to close my FCNR deposit when I come home? +
No — and you usually shouldn't. RBI lets an existing FCNR deposit run to its contracted maturity at the agreed rate even after you become resident, and the interest stays tax-free while you're RNOR (Section 10(15)(iv)(fa)). It's often the cleanest tax outcome a returning officer has, which is why an FCNR deposit is usually the last account you touch. At maturity, move the proceeds into RFC to keep the foreign currency.
