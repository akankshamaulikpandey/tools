Residency checker for merchant navy officers

Are you an NRI this year?

For a seafarer, the answer isn't obvious — and it isn't the same as your bank's answer. Three quick questions, counted the way the law actually counts them. About 90 seconds.

▶ Why isn't this obvious?
Because you have two residencies, not one. The Income-tax Act decides your status by the days you spend in India this year — but for a seafarer, the days you're on an eligible international voyage don't count, and they're read off your CDC, not your passport. FEMA — the law behind your NRE account — decides it differently again, by intention and last year. This tool works out both, and shows you where they don't line up. That gap is where most officers get caught.

Your days in India this tax year

FY 2026-27 · 1 Apr 2026 – 31 Mar 2027

▶ Why my CDC and not my passport?
Because of Rule 126 of the Income-tax Rules. For a crew member, the days you spend on an eligible voyage — an international run between an Indian port and a foreign one — don't count as days in India, and the law measures them from the join and sign-off dates in your Continuous Discharge Certificate. It's the one place the tax code writes a rule just for seafarers. Most calculators — and most banks — miss it and count your passport, which can wrongly tip you into "resident." If a voyage began or ended at a foreign port, your passport stamps are the better record — mark it below and use those dates.

▶ The 182-day rule, and why it's really 182 for you
Anyone is a resident if they're in India 182 days or more in the year. There's a second test — 60 days this year plus 365 across the last four — but because you left India for employment as a seafarer, that 60-day limb is stretched to 182 for you (Section 6). So in practice one line decides it: 182 days in India. Stay under, and you're a non-resident for the year.

Step 3 of 3

The last few years

These decide whether a "resident" year makes you a full resident — or just RNOR, with your foreign income still largely out of Indian tax.

Were you a non-resident (NRI) in at least 9 of the last 10 years?
▶ Why this matters
This is the first RNOR test. If you were a non-resident in 9 of the last 10 years, then even in a year you tip into "resident," you're only RNOR — and most of your foreign income still stays out of Indian tax. Years at sea usually stack up in your favour here.
Roughly how many days were you in India across the last 7 years?
▶ Why this matters
The second RNOR test, and you only need to pass one. 729 days or fewer in India across the last seven years also makes you RNOR rather than a full resident. Seven years of rotations rarely add up past that line.
Is your India-source income above ₹15 lakh this year?
Your ship salary earned abroad is not India-source. This means rent, Indian interest, Indian capital gains, and the like.
Do you pay tax on your income in another country?
▶ Why we ask
Two rules catch high earners. If your Indian income crosses ₹15 lakh, a shorter 120-day line can make you resident (Section 6(2)(b)); and if on top of that you pay tax nowhere, you can be deemed resident — though still only as RNOR. Most officers earn abroad and sit under ₹15 lakh of Indian income, so this usually doesn't apply.
