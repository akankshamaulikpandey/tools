Coming-ashore tax window

When does your RNOR window close?

When you come ashore for good, you get a stretch of two to three years — RNOR — where your foreign income stays outside Indian tax while you reorganise. It closes on a date you can work out. Two questions, about a minute.

▶ What is this window, exactly?
When an NRI returns to India for good, they don't become a full resident overnight. For two to three years they're Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident — RNOR — and through it, foreign income is largely outside India's tax net and interest on a Resident Foreign Currency (RFC) account stays tax-free. It's the single most valuable planning window a returning seafarer gets, and it closes on a fixed date under Section 6(6). Miss it and worldwide income becomes taxable from the first year home.

How we worked this out

Before it closes
The order that protects the window — foreign income shielded, RFC interest tax-free — while it's open.
  1. Redesignate your NRE and NRO accounts
    Do it promptly on return (commonly within a month or so). Under FEMA you're resident from the day you land, and operating an NRE account past that is a contravention.
  2. Open an RFC account before you need it
    Foreign remittances after return go into RFC, which keeps money in foreign currency — and its interest is tax-free through your RNOR years.
  3. Let NRE and FCNR fixed deposits run to maturity
    RBI lets them run to their contracted maturity even after you're resident. FCNR is the one wrapper that survives the change untouched — a reason to fund one before you fly home.
  4. Use the window while it's open
    Crystallise gains that would be taxed once you're ROR, bring foreign assets in order, and set up what comes next — before the clock runs out and the whole world becomes taxable.
